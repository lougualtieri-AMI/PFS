Start here

new? read this first · click to open

You're looking at a planning sketch of your household's money over the years ahead — before and after retirement. Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere.

  1. See it work first. Pick a household from the “— sample households —” menu just below. The whole page fills in: where you stand today, what your savings can safely support each year, and what shifts under different choices.
  2. Read down the page. Where you are (today) → What it supports (a safe yearly spend) → What could happen (retire earlier, spend differently, move, sell the house, plan around taxes).
  3. Make it yours. Edit any number in place, or paste in your own data. Hover — or tap, on a phone — the underlined items and info marks for plain-language explanations.

On a phone? Turn on your browser's “Desktop site” so the wide tables fit on one screen.

Your privacy, plainly. The sample households are completely made up — invented so the app could be built and tested on realistic situations, never on real data. Anything you enter stays only in your browser, on your own computer: it is never sent anywhere, never saved to a server, and never seen by the developer or by the AI that helped build the app. Even the developer's own real finances stayed on his own machine the whole time — everything here was built and checked using only these made-up samples.

Ask an AI how this app works

hand the app to Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT — without handing over your data

Written instructions can only go so far. If something here is unclear — how a tax is figured, what a setting really does, why a number moved — you can give the app itself to an AI assistant and just ask. It will read the whole thing, including the developer's own notes on what the model does and, as importantly, what it deliberately does not try to model.

You are handing over the machine, not your money. This file contains the calculator and nothing else. Your figures are never in it — they live only in your browser, and in the JSON file you export. So you can ask an AI anything you like about how this works without revealing a single dollar of your own situation.

Path 1 — “How does this thing work?”

Hand over the app and ask. Good for questions about the machinery: how a tax is figured, what a setting does, why a number moved.

Path 2 — “What about a situation like mine?”

This is the useful one, and it is not obvious. You can ask an AI about a situation that mirrors yours — closely enough to be worth something — without ever telling it a real number about yourself.

  1. Build a stand-in. Start from a sample household and edit it into the shape of the question you care about — roughly the right ages, roughly the right mix of accounts, a pension that behaves like yours. Round everything. Make the numbers near-misses, not your actual figures. The shape is what matters; the exact dollars are not.
  2. Export it. Use Save JSON below — you now have a small file describing that made-up household.
  3. Hand the AI both files — the app and the JSON — and ask your question. It can now answer about that specific scenario, because it can see both the inputs and the machinery that acts on them.

A stand-in household is not a lesser tool — it is how you get a specific answer while keeping your own figures to yourself. Nothing you enter here ever leaves your browser in the first place; this simply keeps it that way when you go and ask someone else.
